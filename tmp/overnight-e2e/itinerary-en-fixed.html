🌍 Porto, Portugal

Porto is one of those cities that earns its reputation on the ground — strollable, richly tiled, layered with Baroque architecture along the Douro River. For a culture-first traveler who also surfs and wants to cook with locals, three days here hits differently than a generic city break. You're close enough to Madrid (just over an hour's flight) to make this a genuinely restorative long weekend without burning half of it in transit.

📅 Duration & Pace

3 days, balanced rhythm. Mornings are active — surf or culture — afternoons slow down with food and neighborhoods, evenings are short and intentional. No day-trips needed; Porto itself is enough. Build in one flexible half-day to follow your instincts.

✈️ Getting There

Fly from Madrid Barajas (MAD) to Porto Francisco Sá Carneiro (OPO). Iberia, Vueling and Ryanair all run this route — budget £40–80 return if booked 2–4 weeks out, more on summer weekends. Flight time: ~1h10m. From the airport, the Metro Line E (Violet) drops you in central Porto or Matosinhos in about 30–40 minutes for under €2. Skip the taxis at the terminal.

🏨 Where to Stay

Harbour Inn Design Townhouse — Matosinhos

A genuine boutique option in Matosinhos — design-forward rooms in a townhouse format, a short walk from the beach and the seafood strip on Rua Roberto Ivens. Prices typically run £70–95/night. Staying in Matosinhos puts you in a quiet, local spot near the beach, with multiple metro and bus stops connecting you to central Porto or the airport in minutes. Staying here rather than the historic center saves you 20–30% on accommodation while giving you surf access at your doorstep — exactly the trade-off that makes sense for your profile.

We're suggesting Matosinhos over the city center because your priority is price over central location, and with the metro right there, you lose nothing and gain a genuinely local neighborhood vibe.

Ocean Boutique Guesthouse — Matosinhos

A recently renovated bed and breakfast in Matosinhos where guests can make use of free bikes and a garden. Slightly smaller and more personal than Harbour Inn — good pick if you want breakfast included and a slower morning start. Rates around £60–80/night. Book direct when possible.

📍 Day by Day

Day 1 — Tiles, Towers & the Douro at Dusk

Morning: Arrive early, drop your bag at Matosinhos, and take the metro straight to central Porto. Start at São Bento Station — not for the trains, but for the azulejos. The 20,000 hand-painted tiles depicting Portuguese history are one of the most overlooked art installations in Europe; spend 20 minutes here before the tour groups arrive. Then walk uphill to Igreja dos Clérigos: this Baroque tower, built between 1754 and 1763, stands 75 meters tall with 225 steps and offers some of the best views of the city. Admission is €6 and includes access to the museum showcasing Porto's cultural and religious history.

Afternoon: Cross Ponte Dom Luís I on foot to Vila Nova de Gaia and visit the World of Wine (WOW) complex. A set of old Port wine cellars transformed into a 35,000 square metre space with several museums — including the Wine Experience, which journeys through Portuguese wine regions, and Porto Region Across the Ages, which traces the city's historical development. Pick one or two museums rather than rushing through all of them.

Evening: Back in Matosinhos, walk the coastal promenade toward Foz. The wide pedestrian promenade runs along the entire Matosinhos coast with beaches, snack shops, excellent seafood restaurants, and cafés lining the route.

Where to eat: O Gaveto (Rua Roberto Ivens, Matosinhos) — one of the oldest and most respected seafood houses in the area. Order the grilled spider crab or percebes (barnacles) if in season. Budget £25–35pp with wine. Arrive before 8pm or book ahead.

💡 Local tip: Pick up a Porto Card on Day 1 — a 3-day pass runs €25 and covers free or discounted entry to major museums plus unlimited public transport, which includes your metro rides to Matosinhos.

Day 2 — Surf at Dawn, Cook by Afternoon

Morning: Early surf session at Matosinhos Beach. Matosinhos features a consistent beach break suitable for both beginner and intermediate surfers, while the nearby reef provides more of a challenge. If you want to push your level, just north lies Leça da Palmeira, a more powerful and consistent surf spot with longer stretches and slightly harsher waves — a favourite among locals. Rent a board from Siesta Surf School (based in Matosinhos, ~€30 for board + wetsuit) or book a guided session if you want to work on technique.

Afternoon: The culture-meets-kitchen highlight of your trip — a Portuguese cooking class. Cooking Porto runs market-to-table classes starting at Bolhão Market, where you shop with the chef, then cook traditional dishes like bacalhau à Brás or caldo verde. Classes run 3–4 hours and cost €65–80pp. Book in advance; spots fill fast. This one's on the itinerary because your profile explicitly flags cooking classes as a "yes" for this trip — and combining it with a market visit turns it into cultural fieldwork, not just a cooking lesson.

Evening: Low-key. Grab a glass of vinho verde at Base Garden Bar (behind Clérigos Tower) — an open-air garden bar right behind the tower, lovely for a relaxed afternoon drink with a view of the church. It feels like a garden escape in the center of the city. Then walk Rua das Flores back toward the river for dinner.

Where to eat: Taberna Dos Mercadores (Rua dos Mercadores 36, Ribeira) — honest petiscos in a stone-walled taverna. Order the roasted octopus with sweet potato and the arroz de pato. Around £20–25pp. Cash-friendly.

💡 Local tip: Bolhão Market reopened after full renovation — go at 9am on a weekday for the real thing. Saturday is hectic but atmospheric; Sunday it's closed.

Day 3 — Serralves, Fado & the Tram Back to the Ocean

Morning: Take the metro or an Uber (€6 from Matosinhos) to Fundação Serralves. Serralves is a 3.5-hectare park in the middle of Porto featuring a contemporary art museum and the Casa de Serralves, designed by architect Álvaro Siza Vieira. Visiting on a Sunday morning is particularly worthwhile as entrance to the museum is often free. This is your art museum tick — focused on contemporary Portuguese art, which aligns with your museum_type preference for "arte." Budget 2–2.5 hours.

Afternoon: Take Tram Line 1 (Elétrico) from Passeio Alegre toward Foz do Douro. Hop on near the riverfront and ride toward the ocean — it's a slow, scenic historic tram ride along the river, and you can get off at the Foz lighthouse or stop at the Porto Tram Museum halfway. Spend the last hour back at Matosinhos Beach — no board needed, just the Atlantic.

Evening: Attend a traditional Portuguese fado concert — fado is emotional, intimate, and deeply connected to Portuguese culture, and several venues in Porto offer live performances in historic settings. Try Casa da Guitarra (Rua de Santa Catarina) for an authentic, non-touristy session. Entry €15–20, usually includes a drink. Book the night before.

Where to eat: Pre-fado dinner at Café Santiago (Rua Passos Manuel) — home of Porto's most iconic francesinha, the city's signature meat-and-cheese sandwich drowning in spiced tomato-beer sauce. It's a rite of passage. ~€12–15 per person.

💡 Local tip: The Portuguese Center of Photography (Centro Português de Fotografia) is housed in an old prison — the haunting space makes the visit worth it alone, and it's completely free with great views from the upper floors. Fit it in between Serralves and the tram if you have the energy.

⚠️ What Nobody Tells You

  • Porto is hilly and the cobblestones are brutal when wet — bring actual grip on your soles. After rain, the Ribeira streets turn into an ice rink.
  • Matosinhos surf can be deceptively powerful even on calm-looking days — always check Windguru or Surf-Forecast the morning of. The swell direction from the NW can jump fast.
  • The francesinha debate is real: locals will tell you Café Santiago is the original; others swear by A Regaleira two blocks away. Try both if you have the appetite.
  • Fado in Porto ≠ Fado in Lisbon — Porto's version (fado de coimbra influence) is darker and less commercial. Avoid the tourist-trap dinner-fado combos near the waterfront; Casa da Guitarra is the real deal.
