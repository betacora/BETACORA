🌊 Porto — Three Days, Zero Tourist Traps

Porto is compact and walkable, with historic charm, river views, and a wine culture that runs deeper than the port wine cellars across the Douro. For a solo traveler with your profile — culture, surf, food, and genuine local contact — three days here is a surgical strike. You don't need more time. You need the right map.

📅 Duration & Rhythm

3 days, each built around one core mission: Day 1 is arrival and city immersion, Day 2 is surf and seafood out in Matosinhos, Day 3 is deep culture and your cooking class send-off. Balanced pace — mornings are active, afternoons flow where they need to go.

✈️ How to Get There

From Madrid, Porto is one of Europe's most underrated short-haul routes. Vueling and Ryanair both fly direct from MAD to OPO in roughly 1h 45m. Expect to pay £35–£80 return if you book 3–6 weeks out — Tuesday and Wednesday departures tend to run cheaper. Skip the airport express bus and take the Metro Line E directly to the city centre (Trindade station) for €2.60 — it's reliable, well-signposted, and the smarter move.

📍 Day by Day

Day 1 — Arrival, Azulejos & the First Francesinha

Morning: Land, metro to Trindade, check in. Then walk straight to Igreja de Santo Ildefonso — the blue-tile-covered church facing Praça da Batalha is one of the most striking façades in Europe and far less crowded than it should be. From there, cut through the Bairro da Sé neighbourhood on foot, downhill toward the river. Don't rush it.

Afternoon: The Ribeira district is Porto's historic heart and a UNESCO World Heritage site — walk the Douro riverfront, explore medieval streets, and take in the colorful houses facing the river. Then cross Dom Luís I Bridge on the upper deck to Vila Nova de Gaia and visit Taylor's Port Wine Cellars — one of the oldest and most serious lodges in Gaia. The guided tour + tasting runs around £12 and beats every generic wine-bar experience in the city.

Night: Back in Porto proper, head to Rua de Cedofeita or Rua das Flores for a low-key aperitivo — grab a glass of Vinho Verde at a terrace bar and watch the neighbourhood move.

Where to eat: Hit Cervejaria Brasão for one of the best francesinhas in town — the francesinha is Porto's legendary sandwich: steak, ham, and sausage, topped with cheese and a spicy beer sauce, usually served with fries. Rich, filling, best at a specialist café. Budget around £12–15 including a beer. This is non-negotiable on Day 1.

💡 Tip local: The upper deck of Dom Luís I Bridge is free and gives you the best unobstructed view of both banks at sunset — but everyone heads there after dinner. Go at 6pm and you'll essentially have it to yourself.

Day 2 — Waves, Seafood & the Atlantic Coast

Morning: Metro Line B to Matosinhos (30 min from Trindade, €1.85). Matosinhos Beach, just a couple of kilometres from Porto's city centre, is a paradise for surfers. If you want guided time in the water, check out Fish Surf School and Matosinhos Surf Camp for sessions. Both offer 2-hour lessons for around £30–45 including board and wetsuit — book 48h ahead especially on weekends. Conditions on the Atlantic here are real surf, not beginner foam — expect solid beach breaks, best mid-morning before onshore winds pick up.

Afternoon: Post-surf, there's a wide pedestrian promenade running all along the Matosinhos coast, with seafood restaurants, bars, and cafés lining the walk. At Mercado de Matosinhos, locals buy fresh seafood and the nearby restaurants grill it up to perfection. Pick any of the no-frills restaurants around the market — order whatever's on the grill board. This is how locals eat on a Sunday.

Night: Back in Porto, wander the Cedofeita / Bonfim area. Porto's artsy Cedofeita district is packed with independent boutiques, from handmade ceramics to vintage finds. Low-key night — you've got a big morning tomorrow.

Where to eat lunch: A Marisqueira de Matosinhos — order the grilled octopus and the mixed seafood platter. Arrive before 1pm to grab a table without waiting. Around £18–25 per person. Bring cash.

💡 Tip local: After surfing, the locals go to the small café kiosks on the promenade for a bifana (pork sandwich on a roll) and a Sagres — not the restaurants. It's £3 and it hits perfectly with salt water in your hair.

Day 3 — Museums, Market & Cooking Your Way Out

Morning: Start at Museu Nacional Soares dos Reis — Portugal's oldest national museum, packed with 19th-century Portuguese painting and sculpture. Not a tourist circus, genuinely good art. €5 entry, usually quiet before 11am. Then walk 10 minutes to Mercado do Bolhão — an eclectic local market full of fresh produce, meats, fish, bread, flowers, and local handicrafts, housed in an old Neoclassical building that's been here since 1914.

Afternoon: Your headline activity — Canto Cooking Class (Canto de Luz, Porto). It's a 4-hour culinary journey that starts with a guided stroll through Mercado do Bolhão to select fresh local produce, then moves into hands-on cooking guided by a chef. Most Porto cooking schools instruct in English, Portuguese, and Spanish — no language barrier. Cost: around £55–70 per person including wine. Book at least a week out. This one's here because your profile flags cocina_c as a firm "yes" for this trip, and the market-to-kitchen format directly feeds your appetite for local immersion and authentic cultural contact — exactly the kind of experience you won't find by walking into a random restaurant.

Night: You'll leave the cooking class full and with recipes. If you have energy left, Rua Galeria de Paris or Rua de Cândido dos Reis (the core of Porto's bar scene) are 10 minutes away on foot — go for one last glass of port or a ginjinha before the flight home. Don't over-plan this. Let it happen.

Where to eat dinner (if needed): Taberna dos Mercadores near Praça da Ribeira — classic Portuguese dishes in a cozy setting. Bacalhau à Brás or the house stew. Around £20 with wine.

💡 Tip local: For live arts, check Casa da Música's cultural calendar before your trip — if there's a classical or jazz show on Day 1 or Day 3 evening, it's worth the detour. Some events are free.

⚠️ What Nobody Tells You

  • Porto's hills are real. The Ribeira-to-Sé climb will humble you on Day 1 if you're carrying a bag. Pack light and wear actual shoes, not trainers with no grip — cobblestones get slippery.
  • Matosinhos surf on weekends fills up fast. Fish Surf School and Matosinhos Surf Camp both sell out on Saturday mornings. Book Thursday at the latest.
  • Bolhão Market is best before 10am. After that it's tourist-heavy and the best produce is gone. If your cooking class starts with a market visit, they'll time it right — trust the chef.
  • Card payments are widely accepted everywhere in Porto except the smaller market stalls and kiosk cafés on the Matosinhos promenade — carry €20–30 cash for those moments.
